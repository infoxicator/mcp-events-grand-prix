# Race strategy checklist

Open **Strategy replays** from MCP Grand Prix or visit `/?strategy`. The local scenarios run without credentials. Configure Jev and the race engineer in `.env.local` using `.env.local.example`, then start the Node game server to enable **Run live Jev benchmark** and **Run live ChatGPT benchmark**.

| Design item | Where to find it |
| --- | --- |
| Driver horizon, inputs and controls | `server/race-brains.ts` gives Jev speed, track position, heading, curvature, grip, rival positions and closing speed. In the corner benchmark, Jev chooses the line and pedal inputs every 200 ms. The simulator applies steering, throttle and brake through the vehicle physics. |
| Strategist horizon, inputs and outputs | `server/race-brains.ts` supplies lap times, the last three sector gap estimates ahead and behind, tyre age and wear, charge and projected recovery, fuel at the flag, nearby traffic, weather outlook, track wetness and laps remaining. The strategy scenarios add the relevant gap trends, recharge, projected fuel, tyre wear and weather observations. `strategy-radio.ts` records priority, resource budget, target sector, expiry and revisit condition. Multiplayer energy orders carry a budget and expiry and only supply power in the target zone when Jev requests it. A renewed call shares the original spend. The pit cases evaluate the next eligible window. |
| Timing and radio handoff | `server/race-room.ts` revisits the engineer call at the line, when a rival pits, when the car takes damage or when a live spectator changes race conditions. The weather scenario revisits the call after a timed intermediate split arrives. The driver can change line, brake earlier or back out within a call. The live pit wall shows the commitment point on the circuit map, shows distance to the marker and defers late box calls to the next available lap. Outdated driver and engineer responses cannot override newer calls or snapshots. |
| One corner, two approaches | **Corner judgement** keeps initial speed, tyres, grip and the push call fixed and moves the rival off or onto the inside. It overlays both Jev paths and first braking points alongside the fixed-line controller. It records contact, track-limit excursions, time outside the limits, missed deadlines, applied controls, unavailable responses and time to a fixed point on the exit straight. The live run measures provider response time and uses local controls if Jev misses a deadline or a response is unavailable. |
| One boost, two rivals | **One boost, two rivals** starts from P3 with two laps and one boost. Only P4’s closing speed and its resulting gap history differ. ChatGPT sees the last three sector gaps, zones, charge and recharge before the driver runs. An attack spends the charge that might otherwise defend P3. The call displays its sector, budget, expiry and review trigger. The selected and alternate calls use the same local driver and paired seeds. Both calls buy the same speed increment. A separate replay shows the defence call releasing its reserved boost when the rear threat recedes. The screen shows both gap trends, radio, deployment point, place and finishing gaps, with head-to-head counts for place and equal-place gap comparisons. |
| Fuel by sector | **Fuel by sector** compares projected fuel at the flag, savings and time cost by sector. The selected two-lap lift keeps full pace on the main straight and shows the resulting fuel margin and time loss, including an uncovered deficit. |
| Fight or yield | **Fight or yield** weighs rival pace, tyre-age advantage, time lost defending and the gap to the group. It shows P3 or P4 and the group gap after the move and any slipstream gain. |
| Push or manage tyres | **Push or manage tyres** evaluates the current gap against extra wear and late-stint pace. It shows the next gap, wear and expected late loss. The push call expires at the next lap line. |
| Box, extend or cover | **Box, extend or cover** compares pit loss, fresh-tyre pace and rejoin traffic. It shows box, extend and cover calls with on-time and late receipt relative to a visible commitment marker. |
| Weather tyre choice | **Weather tyre choice** shows sector grip, forecast arrival range, intermediate inventory and a timed intermediate split. The call changes from slicks to intermediates when the evidence supports it and shows the tyre and set count. In live races, spectators can select a rain intensity. The strategist sees track wetness and can fit slicks, intermediates or wets with the usual pit commitment. |
| Teammate coordination | **Teammate coordination** compares tyre life, pace and projected team points with a second team car in the scenario. It shows the swap, reversal when no pass is made in two laps, and points when the pass succeeds. |
| Pit strategy extension | **Pit strategy benchmark** holds tyre age and pace fixed and varies the rear gap. It compares boxing now and extending in each variant at the same sector-three timing gate after the second lap’s stop, when Jev and the rival have both stopped. ChatGPT selects a plan for each saved state. Its measured response time maps to a receipt point on the pit approach. The replay reports receipt relative to both commitment and pit entry. |

The corner uses the game’s vehicle physics. The battery and pit benchmarks use longitudinal timing models. Fuel, fight or yield, tyre management, weather and teammate coordination run in isolated strategy scenarios. Live provider tests use fixture responses so their results are reproducible.

For native race telemetry and radio in a ChatGPT Work chat, use `open_grand_prix_strategy` or **Open ChatGPT Work strategy lobby**. Subscribe with the event filter and click **Start race** in the Ferrari widget. The public race runs at 1× for 20 laps or until you stop it. Ferrari starts solo. The Mercedes strategy invitation adds a second player with their own telemetry and radio in the same race. Paid Jev driving requires Cloudflare Access, a verified subscription for ChatGPT strategy, and an explicit start.

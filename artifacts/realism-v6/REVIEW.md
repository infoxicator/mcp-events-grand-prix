## Reference measurements and silhouette

`reference-measurements.jpg` annotates manually selected approximate pixel bounds in video 00:01 (1440×1080). Airbox mouth: x536–575, y255–332, approximately 39×77 pixels. Nearby rear tyre: y314–485, approximately 171 pixels tall; apparent opening/tyre height ≈0.45. The baseline authored opening/tyre ratio was 0.204/0.72≈0.283, visibly squatter in the saved front capture; comparing that metric ratio with the projected video ratio remains approximate. These are image-space estimates: cameras, foreshortening and depth differ, so this is a useful silhouette guide, not a metric reconstruction. The annotated 171×82 sidepod region includes the oblique mouth AND undercut; it is not an aperture cross-section measurement.

Airbox changed from a 0.204 m round black disc to a 0.318 m tall, tapered recessed mouth with horizontal and lower divided passages. Reference ratio suggests roughly 0.32 m for a 0.72 m tyre at similar depth. Sidepod lip widened from 0.414 to 0.464 m, mouth height 0.086→0.098 m; surrounding leading sections widened to support the integrated lip. Kept the reference's black halo, white forward airbox/cockpit, red rear fin/body and carbon aero pieces, with fictional brands only.

`measurements.json` is computed from actual exported GLB positions and hierarchy by `scripts/measure-car.py`; unit forearm sleeves are excluded because runtime poses them. The audit found hardware extending past the nominal 2 m width, so wheel assemblies moved inward slightly (front 17.5 mm, rear 27.5 mm per side). Wheelbase stays 3.60 m; length approximately 5.63 m. Nose tip ~0.18 m, nose over front axle ~0.46 m, rear-wing top ~0.964 m were retained: the video does not justify changing these from unmatched perspective views. ## First implementation

Studio reflection rig now has a broad overhead panel, narrow side strip and rear rim panel, with lower PMREM blur. Satin base roughness retained; clearcoat raised moderately with a sharper coat lobe. This is an original analytic softbox environment matched by eye, not the unavailable HDRI from the video. Floor reflection remains blurred and inexpensive, with Blender-baked contact AO.

Ten independent Blender finger pivots, varied lengths/curls and an opposed thumb replace the fixed joined finger pose. Runtime changes grip pressure subtly by steering direction and digit, with a tiny deterministic motion during travel. Palms stay on grips, forearms continue aiming from stable elbows. This is articulated geometry, not cloth/skin simulation. Added asset rig and deterministic motion checks.

Monza adds denser canopy placement and a lower stratum of clustered shrubs/saplings using existing instanced crown meshes. Reduced prototype variations from four to two per species and shortened near-detail LOD to 115 m to offset added vegetation. ## Screenshot review and correction

Inspected the complete first-round car/contact sheets, including front, side, rear, wheel, steering, Clay/Wireframe, chase, onboard/T-cam and both steering locks. Added dedicated airbox and sidepod close-ups rather than judging the openings only from the wide view. Compared front/rear/steering/wheel against video 00:01, 00:18, 00:05.25 and 00:04.25. The first export's lip floated ahead of a mismatched rectangular cutout and its fictional blue badge partly intersected the enlarged body. Rejected that fit: extended the fairing, cut the tapered mouth profile through the shell, added a small camera pedestal, projected the badge onto the finished skin, relaxed decimation ripples and added small physical aperture edge radii. Sidepod cutter now matches the wider mouth instead of leaving red end plugs. Fingers received tapered sections and a darker, rougher textile finish.

Inspected all four shots per circuit and the extra Strip aerial. Monza's finer foliage texture reads less like coarse pebbles, and the lower vegetation adds depth, but the aerial still shows substantial grass gaps and solid crown shapes. Pit buildings/crowds remain simplified. Vegas's curved façade and fountain layout are recognizable additions, but the first windows-only material looked like the surrounding black-glass boxes. Added an original warm limestone/recessed-window atlas and moderate warm emission, preserving the actual geometry. The Sphere-zone aerial is largely unchanged because these new landmarks are on the Strip, not beside the Sphere. Photo comparison cameras are not matched; no pixel-accurate architectural claim.

The second capture set (`iteration-2/`) was rejected too: the hand-built tapered cutter had inward normals, so Boolean subtraction left an obvious white cap across the airbox mouth. The badge's nearest-surface projection collapsed both sides of an ellipsoid onto each other. Fixed the cutter normals explicitly before Boolean operations; sidepod cutters now use the actual elliptical mouth profile rather than oversized boxes. Replaced the badge with one ray-projected surface sheet and separately projected lettering. The queued verification was stopped before benchmarks; complete checks are repeated on the corrected final export. Also cached the ten finger-node bindings at model load, avoiding repeated recursive scene searches every frame without changing animation.

Third inspection confirmed the capped mouth and folded badge were fixed. It still exposed small white slivers between the smooth intake lip and its coarse polygonal cutout. Resampled the same closed profile for the duct and cutter to reduce that mismatch. Kept these close-ups in the evidence; they also show that the halo roots and fairing transitions remain less polished than the reference even after the functional geometry defects are corrected. The studio floor was darkened and carbon environment response reduced after the brighter softbox pass made the carbon bands too grey.

## Final visual assessment

Inspected every final car view/contact sheet and the paired front, rear, wheel and steering reference sheets, plus the final inlet close-ups. The final mouth is open and its black lining follows the smooth cutout; the blue badge is a single surface sheet. The measured airbox silhouette and wider sidepod opening are closer to the video. The studio has clearer edge highlights while retaining satin paint. Wheelbase/overall dimensions are audited rather than inferred from the camera. Clay and wireframe confirm the actual model topology; both steering locks retain hand contact, and individual finger motion is bounded by tests.

It is still visibly short of the reference: halo roots and cockpit coaming retain uneven close-up transitions, the sidepod lip is heavier than the video's integrated opening, and the nose/front-wing profile and rear-wing/endplate shapes are interpretations. The video’s tyre lettering and dish gradients are finer; ours has simpler rubber/sidewall finish. The wheel's controls remain larger/coarser and its cockpit lining less finely textured. Fingers now move independently but are simplified sculpted forms with no skin deformation or cloth wrinkles. Mirrors still share one feed. The video contains no driving onboard, so onboard framing is assessed against the baseline and visibility requirements, not claimed as a camera-matched reference.

Inspected all final Monza and Vegas chase/onboard/aerial/pit images against the existing real-location photographs, plus the new Strip view. Monza has more depth at ground level and finer leaf texture, but still uses solid crown masses and has grass gaps unlike dense real woodland; pit architecture and crowds remain simplified. Vegas's warm curved façade, pavilions, fountain rings and retained Sphere/tower improve recognition. The real Strip photo still has much denser low-level activity, richer façade detail, brighter fountain spray and more complex warm light spill. Our fountain jets are static meshes, not fluid simulation. Reflections are inexpensive probe/planar approximations without surface-specific parallax or ray tracing. Photo cameras are unmatched; geometry is not a surveyed replica.

No Blender runtime crash/hang occurred this round. Used 5.2.2 LTS with factory startup and CPU Cycles AO; the fixes were modelling/export defects, not a procedural Three.js fallback. Rejected captures and their logs remain in the round folder. Circuit images copied into the car-only third review set are the unchanged second circuit capture, not claimed as a separate circuit rerender.

A local 64px cube probe captures actual nearby buildings/lights every 240 m for wet-look asphalt and wall response. This is a low-resolution local environment approximation, not screen-space or ray-traced reflections.

## Remaining fidelity limits

- The nose/deck/sidepod/engine skin is connected, and the major overlapping white-body seams are removed. Its contours are still inferred: the video has a broader nose/front-wing outer profile, more integrated intake lip and subtler airbox/halo-root transitions. Floor-edge aero pieces, wings and halo remain separate manufactured components. The paint boundary is clean geometry, but its exact shape differs from the Ferrari.
- Wheel dishes reveal their rounded inner finish now. Brake hardware is intentionally behind an opaque cover; most is visible only inboard. Tyre deformation remains a bounded visual effect, not a carcass solver. Studio uses softbox environment lighting, baked AO and a planar floor reflection, without path-traced contact gradients.
- Wrists/forearms articulate with steering, and the fingers have a tighter grip pose. Fingers do not articulate independently; textile wrinkles, thumb anatomy and sleeve creasing remain simplified. Both mirrors still share one rear feed. The reference video has no driving onboard, so those views are assessed for framing/visibility and against our baseline, not claimed as a matched video camera.
- Monza’s species proportions and planting vary more, but foliage is still solid textured lobes rather than individual leaves, with sparse undergrowth and approximate species distribution. Earlier LOD/shadow cutoffs are visible tradeoffs for three-view performance.
- Vegas has substantially more urban massing, varied towers and lit signs. It is still fictional architecture around the correct circuit, not a surveyed replica of the Strip. Wet-look reflections use a low-resolution local cube probe and point-light highlights, with no per-surface parallax correction or ray tracing; buildings can shift in reflections when the probe refreshes. Weather/physics are unchanged.

Final contact-sheet inspection covered every saved car pose (including left/right lock), both four-view circuit sets and the extra Strip aerial. The final white paint edge is clean, the red inner cockpit panels are now carbon, the wheel dish has a visible inner highlight, and hands remain attached through steering. Reopened video frames 00:02/00:03/00:04/00:06/00:09/00:18 to check Clay, Wireframe, cockpit and rear proportions. The final Strip comparison especially shows the remaining gap: our added towers improve density, but the real photograph has much brighter warm façade lighting, landmark-specific architecture and ground-level activity. Circuit-photo cameras are explicitly labelled unmatched; comparisons are qualitative, not pixel-aligned accuracy evidence.

## Performance refinement

First isolated final-model measurement: Monza 92.5 / 91.9 / 93.2 FPS, lowest smoothed sample 75.2; Vegas 104.6 / 104.5 / 104.4, lowest 89.8. Monza triangle counts fell substantially, but total draw calls remained high. Tried reusing the racing shadow map for alternate rendered frames, retaining every-frame car/camera updates and every-frame showroom shadows. This trades up to one frame of shadow lag for fewer shadow passes; there is no resolution or mesh-detail reduction. Re-benchmark and final regression results are recorded in VERIFICATION.md.

Rejected alternate-frame shadow reuse: Monza remained about 92–93 FPS and its lowest smoothed sample fell to 68.9, so the visual lag had no useful payoff. Reverted it; final racing shadows refresh normally. Next measured static scenery matrix caching: Blender scenery transforms never animate, so their already-computed world matrices can be retained while visibility/LOD/material animation still updates.

Static-matrix caching also showed no useful gain (Monza 91.9 / 91.3 / 91.9 FPS); reverted that experiment too. Final code keeps normal matrix and shadow updates. The approximately 110 FPS stretch target was not reached; the retained improvements are the Blender crown/detail reduction and LOD/shadow-distance tuning. Both experiments are retained as evidence rather than described as successful optimizations.

Final retained-code benchmarks: Monza **90.96 / 90.04 / 91.17 FPS**, minimum smoothed sample **69.60**; Vegas **103.80 / 103.68 / 104.19**, minimum **86.99**. Both meet the test's 60 FPS mean/minimum-smoothed threshold on this M4 Pro; Monza did not reach 110.
